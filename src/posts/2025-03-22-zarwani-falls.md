Shoolpaneshwar Wildlife Sanctuary hides a waterfall that most Vadodara residents have never seen. The forest path to Zarwani is narrow, overgrown in places, and perfect. No signal, no shortcuts, no crowds. Just the sound of water getting louder as you walk.

## Into Shoolpaneshwar

The sanctuary sits about three hours from Vadodara by road. You enter through a small forest department checkpoint — minimal paperwork, a small entry fee, and a reminder to not disturb the wildlife. Given that the sanctuary has leopards, sloth bears, and various things that would rather not be disturbed, this seems like reasonable advice.

The forest closes around you quickly once you're past the entrance. Teak, bamboo, and dense undergrowth on both sides of a narrow track that is technically a road but behaves more like a suggestion.

## The Walk to Zarwani

The path to the falls is well-worn enough to follow but wild enough to keep you paying attention. There are no trail markers, no safety railings, no signboards in English. You navigate by the sound of water and by following the paths that others have followed before you.

## The Falls

Zarwani is not a dramatic waterfall — it doesn't drop 100 metres or thunder. It's a wide, multi-tiered fall over black rock, maybe 15 metres at the main drop, with several smaller cascades below it. In monsoon it would be spectacular. In March it was quieter, the water lower, which meant you could stand on the rocks at the base and look up at it without getting soaked.

We sat there for an hour. Ate lunch. Watched a kingfisher work the pool below the falls. Didn't check our phones because there was no signal and, more importantly, no reason to.

## What Nobody Tells You

The walk back feels longer than the walk in, because you've already used the anticipation. Pack something to eat at the falls and stay a while — it earns the return journey.
