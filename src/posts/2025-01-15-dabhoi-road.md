Left the city at 5 AM, when the streets still belong to stray dogs and milk vans. By the time the sun came up over the fields past Dabhoi, I had already done 40 km and eaten half a pack of biscuits.

Long-distance cycling in Gujarat is not scenic in the postcard sense — it's honest, flat, and quietly magnificent. The road to Dabhoi is a straight line through agricultural land that rewards patience over ambition.

## The Route

The exit from Vadodara toward Dabhoi is unremarkable for the first 15 km. Then the city releases you. Fields open. The sky widens. The pace that felt slow in the city feels exactly right out here.

The road surface is mostly good — a few stretches of broken tarmac past the toll booth that wake you up if you've drifted into autopilot. The truck traffic is manageable before 7 AM. After that, you're sharing the road more honestly.

## Dabhoi Fort

The old stepwell at Dabhoi is worth the detour if you've never seen it. It was built in the 13th century and still holds water in monsoon. I arrived just as a group of school children were filing past — the juxtaposition of modern school uniforms against 800-year-old carved stone is something.

## The Return

I came back the same way, which is usually a mistake on a flat route because there's nothing new to see. But the wind had shifted and was behind me for 30 km, which made me forgive every kilometre.

## What I'd Do Differently

Start 30 minutes earlier. The first light between Dabhoi and the return road is worth being tired for. I also underestimated how quickly the headwind would develop — carry an extra 500ml on this route.
