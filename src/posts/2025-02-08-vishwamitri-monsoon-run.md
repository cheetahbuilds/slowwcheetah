Vadodara's riverfront in monsoon is a different place entirely. The Vishwamitri was running high and the footpath was half-mud, half-puddle. Ran 12 km and came back soaked to the bone. Felt like the best decision I'd made all week.

## Why Run in the Rain

There's a particular kind of stubbornness required to put on running shoes when it's raining. The reward for that stubbornness is having the riverfront almost entirely to yourself. The usual morning crowd — the walkers, the dog owners, the chai drinkers — stay home when it rains. You get the river.

## The Vishwamitri in Full Flow

The crocodiles were out, which they always are after heavy rain — sunning themselves on the far bank, unbothered by the weather or by me running past. I've done this route dozens of times and I still pause to look at them every single time.

The river was brown and fast. The sound of it was different — more urgent than usual. Running alongside moving water when it's actually moving is something else entirely.

## The Mud Problem

The last 3 km of the southern loop get genuinely muddy after rain. Trail shoes would be the right call. I was wearing road shoes and spent those 3 km being careful about my footing. A slow, deliberate 3 km — which, thinking about it, is kind of the whole philosophy of this thing.

## Post-Run

Came home completely soaked, brewed chai, sat by the window watching the rain continue. Some mornings earn the rest of the day more than others.
