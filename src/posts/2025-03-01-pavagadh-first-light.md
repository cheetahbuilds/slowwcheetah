Two hours from Vadodara and you're standing on a UNESCO World Heritage hillside, looking out over the Champaner ruins below. The trek up through the old stone steps in the early morning, with mist still clinging to the forest, is one of those experiences that makes you wonder why you don't do this every weekend.

## Getting There

Left Vadodara at 4:30 AM. The highway at that hour is quiet and slightly eerie. Arrived at the Champaner base around 6:15 — early enough to start the climb before the day got warm, early enough to have the lower steps mostly to ourselves.

## The Climb

The route up Pavagadh mixes ancient stone steps with sections of open hillside. The steps are worn smooth by centuries of pilgrims — not technical, but slippery when wet. The mist was thick in the lower forest sections, which made the surroundings feel genuinely otherworldly.

The Kalikamata temple at the summit draws devotees from across Gujarat. By the time we arrived at the top, a few early pilgrims were already there. The sound of bells and the smell of incense mixing with the cold morning air is a specific kind of beautiful that doesn't photograph well.

## The View

From the top, Champaner spreads out below — the walled city, the mosques, the geometric patterns of the old streets. The Gujarat plains extend beyond it in every direction. At first light, with haze still hanging over everything, it looks like a painting that isn't quite finished.

## Getting Down

The cable car exists and is an option. I've never used it and don't plan to. The descent on foot takes about 90 minutes, passes different sections of the hillside than the ascent, and gives you the whole thing again in reverse.

## What to Bring

Water (more than you think), comfortable shoes with grip, and an empty morning. Don't rush it.
